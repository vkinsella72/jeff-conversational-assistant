# Slow-Cooker Beef in Stout (Beef Stew, Irish-Style)

**Category:** beef
**Source:** Jeff's, 2026-07-22
**Created:** 2026-07-22
**Last made:** —
**Rating:** —
**Servings:** 6
**Prep:** 15 min **Cook:** 8 hours (low) or 4 hours (high) **Total:** 8 hours 15 min

## Quick description

The proper Sunday-winter-slow-cooker-stew. Beef shin or chuck braised in stout, stock, and vegetables until the meat falls apart and the gravy is dark and rich. **Stout is the magic — it adds the depth that makes this restaurant quality.** Mash or chunky bread to soak up the gravy.

## Ingredients

- 1.2 kg beef shin or chuck steak, cut into 4-5 cm chunks
- 3 tbsp plain flour
- Salt and black pepper
- 3 tbsp vegetable oil
- 2 onions, sliced
- 3 carrots, cut into chunks
- 3 celery sticks, cut into chunks
- 4 garlic cloves, crushed
- 1 tbsp tomato purée
- 500 ml stout (Guinness or similar)
- 300 ml beef stock
- 2 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 tsp dried thyme
- 300 g button mushrooms, left whole
- Fresh parsley or dried parsley, to finish

## Method

1. **Coat the beef.** Toss the beef chunks in the flour seasoned with plenty of salt and pepper.
2. **Brown the beef.** Heat 2 tbsp oil in a large frying pan over high heat. Brown the beef in batches (don't crowd) for 2-3 minutes per side until deeply caramelised. Transfer to the slow-cooker.
3. **Sauté the veg.** Reduce heat to medium. Add the remaining oil to the pan. Cook the onions, carrots, and celery for 6-8 minutes until starting to colour. Add the garlic and tomato purée, cook for 1 minute.
4. **Deglaze.** Pour in the stout and bring to a vigorous simmer, scraping up all the brown bits on the bottom. Let it reduce by about a third (5 minutes or so).
5. **Slow-cook.** Tip the veg, stout, beef stock, Worcestershire sauce, bay leaves, and thyme into the slow-cooker. Stir to combine.
6. **Cook on LOW for 8 hours** or **HIGH for 4 hours**, until the beef is falling-apart tender and the gravy is thick and glossy.
7. **Add the mushrooms** in the last 30 minutes of cooking (they go rubbery if added at the start).
8. **Finish.** Fish out the bay leaves. Taste and adjust seasoning. Scatter with parsley.

## Notes / variations

- **Beef shin > beef chuck for this.** Shin has more connective tissue, which breaks down into that silky, fall-apart texture. Chuck is the next-best option.
- **No slow-cooker?** A covered casserole dish in a 160°C oven for 3 hours. Same ingredients, same finish.
- **The flour at the start is doing two jobs:** coating the beef for a better sear AND thickening the gravy. Don't skip it.
- **Stout sub:** if you haven't got stout, any dark beer works. Even a dark ale. The point is the deep, malty flavour.
- **Mash on the side:** proper mash (butter, milk, salt, pepper) is the classic pairing, even though potatoes aren't your go-to. For a weeknight, the leftover gravy is also lush drizzled over rice.

## Pairs with

- Creamy mashed potato
- Buttered green beans or peas
- A pint of the same stout
- Crusty bread to mop the gravy
- The next day's leftovers on toast with a fried egg on top (hash-like)
