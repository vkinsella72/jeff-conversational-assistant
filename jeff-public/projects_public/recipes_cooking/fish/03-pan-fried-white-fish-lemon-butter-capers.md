# Pan-Fried White Fish with Lemon-Butter-Caper Sauce

**Category:** fish
**Source:** Jeff's, 2026-07-22
**Created:** 2026-07-22
**Last made:** —
**Rating:** —
**Servings:** 2
**Prep:** 5 min **Cook:** 15 min **Total:** 20 min

## Quick description

The 20-minute Friday-night treat. White fish (cod, haddock, sea bass — whatever's at the fishmonger) pan-fried until the skin is crisp and the flesh flakes, served with a quick lemon-butter-caper pan sauce. **The fish itself is just cooked simply — the sauce is where the restaurant-quality lives.**

## Ingredients

- 2 white fish fillets (cod, haddock, sea bass, or similar — skin-on if possible)
- Salt and black pepper
- 2 tbsp plain flour (for a light dredge)
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 3 tbsp butter
- 1 small shallot (or ½ small onion), finely chopped
- 3 garlic cloves, crushed
- 2 tbsp capers, drained
- Zest and juice of 1 lemon
- 1 tbsp chopped fresh parsley (or 1 tsp dried)

## Method

1. **Prep the fish.** Pat the fillets dry with kitchen paper (very important — wet fish steams, dry fish crisps). Season both sides with salt and pepper. Lightly dredge in flour, shaking off the excess.
2. **Sear the fish.** Heat the olive oil in a heavy-based frying pan over medium-high heat until shimmering. Lay the fillets in **skin-side down** (or presentation-side down if skinless). **Don't move them for 4 minutes** — the skin needs to crisp and release naturally. If it sticks, it's not ready.
3. **Flip and finish.** When the skin is deeply golden and lifts easily, flip the fillets and cook for another 2-3 minutes on the flesh side until the fish is just cooked through (it should flake easily at the thickest part but still feel slightly bouncy). Transfer to a warm plate, cover loosely.
4. **Pan sauce.** Reduce the heat to medium. Add 1 tbsp of the butter to the same pan. Add the shallot and cook for 2-3 minutes until soft. Add the garlic and capers, cook for 30 seconds.
5. **Deglaze.** Squeeze in the lemon juice, add the zest, and let it bubble for 30 seconds, scraping up the brown bits. Whisk in the remaining 2 tbsp butter off the heat to make a glossy sauce.
6. **Serve.** Spoon the sauce over the fish, scatter with parsley. Goes brilliantly with mashed potato, new potatoes, or rice.

## Notes / variations

- **The skin needs to be dry and the pan needs to be hot.** That's the whole fish-frying game. Wet fish in a cold pan = sad, pale, stuck-on skin.
- **No capers?** Chopped green olives or a splash of white wine vinegar. Different but works.
- **The pan sauce is the restaurant move.** Don't wash the pan before making it — the brown bits are the flavour.
- **No fresh parsley?** Dried parsley (½ tsp) in the sauce is fine.
- **Add to a sandwich:** Flake the fish, pile into a brioche bun with the sauce, some lettuce, a slice of tomato. Friday-night fish butty, upgraded.

## Pairs with

- New potatoes or mashed potato
- Steamed asparagus or tenderstem broccoli
- A glass of cold white wine
- The leftover sauce is brilliant drizzled over vegetables the next day
