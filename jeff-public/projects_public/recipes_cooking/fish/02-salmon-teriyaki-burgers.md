# Salmon Teriyaki Burgers

**Category:** fish
**Source:** the principal's, 2026-07-22 (recreated from their description of the last time)
**Created:** 2026-07-22
**Last made:** —
**Rating:** —
**Servings:** 2 burgers
**Prep:** 20 min **Cook:** 15 min **Total:** 35 min

## Quick description

Pan-seared salmon fillets (just to get the crust), flaked and mixed with panko, egg, and teriyaki, formed into patties with the **salmon skin wrapped around the outside** (this is the move — it binds the burger, crisps up under the broiler, and keeps the omega-3 fats where they belong). Skillet + broiler finish for the char. Brioche bun, lettuce, tomato, burger sauce, extra teriyaki at the table. **Lush.**

## Ingredients

### For the salmon burgers

- 2 salmon fillets, skin-on (around 150 g each, give or take)
- 1 tbsp avocado oil (for the first sear)
- 4-5 tbsp panko breadcrumbs
- 1 large egg
- 2-3 tbsp teriyaki sauce (plus more for brushing and serving)
- Salt and black pepper
- 1 tbsp avocado oil (for the second skillet sear)

### To assemble

- 2 brioche buns
- Lettuce leaves (little gem or baby gem works well)
- 1 tomato, sliced
- Burger sauce (any you like — the shop-bought stuff is fine; Kewpie mayo + sriracha + a squeeze of lime is the cheat version)
- Extra teriyaki sauce, to drizzle

## Method

1. **Get the crust on the fillets first.** Pat the salmon fillets dry. Season lightly with salt and pepper. Heat 1 tbsp avocado oil in a skillet over medium-high heat. Place the fillets **skin-down** and sear for 3-4 minutes until the skin is crisp and the flesh is just cooked through (you'll see the colour change climbing up the side of the fillet). Flip for 30 seconds, then transfer to a plate and let them cool enough to handle. **Keep the skin attached — you're going to need it later.**
2. **Flake the salmon.** Using two forks (or your fingers once it's cool), flake the salmon flesh into a mixing bowl, breaking up any large chunks but leaving some texture — you don't want mush. **Set the skins aside on a separate plate.** They'll be wrapped around the patties.
3. **Make the mix.** To the flaked salmon, add the panko, the egg, and 2-3 tbsp teriyaki. Mix gently with a fork until just combined — over-mixing makes the patties dense. The mix should hold together when you press a bit in your palm; if it's too wet, add another spoonful of panko. If it's too dry, a splash more teriyaki.
4. **Form the patties with the skin.** Divide the mix into 2 equal portions. Shape each into a patty roughly the size of your brioche bun. Take one of the reserved salmon skins and **wrap it around the outside of the patty**, like a belt, pressing gently so it sticks. The skin should cover the sides and a bit of the top/bottom — it'll crisp up under the broiler and hold everything together. Repeat with the second patty. **If the skin tears, just patch it — it'll crisp up fine.**
5. **Skillet sear.** Heat 1 tbsp avocado oil in a clean skillet (cast iron is ideal, but any oven-safe skillet works) over medium-high heat. When the oil shimmers, lay the patties in. Sear for 2-3 minutes per side until the skin-wrapped outside is golden and crisp.
6. **Broiler finish.** Brush the tops of the patties with a little extra teriyaki. Slide the whole skillet under a hot broiler (or transfer to a tray if your skillet handle isn't oven-safe) for 2-4 minutes — you're looking for the teriyaki to bubble, caramelise, and the edges to char slightly. **Watch it like a hawk** — the sugars in teriyaki burn fast.
7. **Toast the buns.** While the patties finish under the broiler, split the brioche buns and toast them cut-side down in a dry pan or under the broiler for 30-60 seconds. Keep an eye on them — brioche burns quickly.
8. **Assemble.** Sauce the bun base. Layer lettuce, then tomato, then the salmon patty (skin-side up so you see the char), then a final drizzle of teriyaki. Sauce the bun lid. Close. Eat immediately while the skin is still crackling.

## Notes / variations

- **The skin wrap is the signature move.** It's what makes these burgers "lush" rather than just "salmon patties" — it binds the burger, crisps up, and keeps the omega-3 fats in. Don't skip it. If a piece of skin tears off mid-cook, just press it back on before the broiler finish.
- **Pan size matters.** Use a skillet that's small enough that the patties aren't swimming in oil, but big enough that they're not touching. You want the sear, not a steam.
- **Panko amount is a feel thing.** Start with 4 tbsp, mix, then judge. Fresh salmon can be wetter than tinned — adjust by the spoonful.
- **Mix, don't knead.** Overworked salmon = rubbery burgers. Fold gently until just combined.
- **Teriyaki in the mix AND on the outside.** Both. The in-the-mix teriyaki is the flavour base, the brushed-on teriyaki is the glaze. They're doing different jobs.
- **No broiler?** Finish the patties in a very hot oven (230°C / 210°C fan) for 3-5 minutes. You'll get a bit less char but still get the caramelisation.
- **Want it spicier?** Add a pinch of chilli flakes to the mix, or use a sriracha-mayo burger sauce.
- **Bun alternatives:** a sesame brioche bun is even better if you can find one. Potato buns work. Ciabatta if you want a more sandwich-y vibe. For low-carb, a large lettuce leaf wrap holds it fine (the skin wrap keeps the patty together).
- **Sides:** sweet potato fries, a simple green salad with sesame dressing, or just crisps. Don't overthink it.

## Pairs with

- A cold lager or a chilled dry white (the teriyaki loves both)
- Sweet potato fries or proper chunky chips
- Pickled cucumber or quick-pickled red onion on the side, if you've got 10 min to spare
- The leftover teriyaki drizzled over leftover patty in tomorrow's lunch rice bowl
