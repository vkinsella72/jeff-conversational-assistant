# Teriyaki Salmon Tray Bake

**Category:** fish
**Source:** Jeff's, 2026-07-22 (built on the salmon teriyaki burger principle, plus the chicken tray-bake muscle memory)
**Created:** 2026-07-22
**Last made:** —
**Rating:** —
**Servings:** 2
**Prep:** 10 min **Cook:** 25 min **Total:** 35 min

## Quick description

Two salmon fillets on a bed of tenderstem broccoli, red pepper, and spring onion whites, drenched in a honey-soy-ginger-garlic teriyaki glaze. The veg goes in first to soak up the salmon fats as they render — that's the whole point. Glaze caramelises on top, salmon stays silky in the middle, broccoli soaks up everything that drips off. One tray, one pan, one round of washing up. **The Omega-3 stays where it is — don't squeeze the fillets.**

## Ingredients

- 2 salmon fillets, skin-on, around 120-150 g each
- 250 g tenderstem broccoli (or regular broccoli cut into florets)
- 1 red pepper, sliced into 2 cm strips
- 4 spring onions, whites sliced into 5 cm pieces, greens kept aside for garnish
- 4 tbsp honey
- 3 tbsp soy sauce (light or dark, both work)
- 1 tbsp mirin (or 1 tbsp rice vinegar + ½ tsp sugar as a sub)
- 1 tbsp sesame oil (or vegetable oil)
- 1 tbsp grated fresh ginger (or 1 tsp dried)
- 3 garlic cloves, grated or crushed
- 1 tsp dried chilli flakes (optional, for a bit of heat)
- 1 tbsp vegetable oil for the tray
- Salt and black pepper
- Toasted sesame seeds, to finish (optional)
- Lemon wedges, to serve

## Method

1. Heat the oven to 220°C (200°C fan). Get a large roasting tray out — one layer, not stacked.
2. In a small bowl, whisk together the honey, soy sauce, mirin (or vinegar + sugar), sesame oil, ginger, garlic, and chilli flakes (if using). This is your teriyaki glaze.
3. Toss the broccoli, red pepper, and spring onion whites on the tray with the vegetable oil, a pinch of salt, and a few grinds of black pepper. Spread in one layer.
4. Roast the veg for **12-15 minutes** on their own — they need a head start so they're properly tender by the time the salmon's done.
5. Pull the tray out. Push the veg to the edges, leaving space in the middle for the salmon.
6. Pat the salmon fillets dry with kitchen paper. Season lightly with salt and pepper. Place them **skin-down** in the middle of the tray, on top of the veg.
7. Spoon about **half the glaze** over the salmon, brushing it across the top and a little down the sides. Reserve the rest.
8. Return to the oven for **10-12 minutes** — the salmon is done when it flakes easily at the thickest part but is still slightly translucent in the very centre (it'll keep cooking as it rests). The glaze should be bubbling and starting to caramelise.
9. Optional: for a proper teriyaki-style glaze set, finish under the grill for 1-2 minutes. **Watch it like a hawk** — honey burns fast.
10. Drizzle the reserved glaze over the salmon and veg. Scatter with the reserved spring onion greens and toasted sesame seeds if using. Serve straight from the tray with a lemon wedge each.

## Notes / variations

- **Salmon is the star, not the sauce.** If your fillets are thicker or thinner, adjust the final cook time. 1.5 cm thick at the centre = 10 min. 2.5 cm thick = 12-14 min. When in doubt, take it out a touch early — it finishes cooking on the tray.
- **No mirin?** The rice vinegar + sugar sub works fine. No sake/mirin in the pantry? Just use 1 tbsp extra honey + a splash of white wine vinegar.
- **Want more veg?** Throw in some sliced chestnut mushrooms with the broccoli. They soak up the salmon fat brilliantly.
- **No tenderstem broccoli?** Use regular broccoli florets, or pointed cabbage in wedges, or pak choi halved lengthways.
- **Skin on or off?** Keep it on. The skin protects the flesh from overcooking and the fat underneath renders into the veg. **Do not squeeze the fillets** — the Omega-3 fats in the salmon are exactly what the veg underneath is catching and turning into flavour.
- **Want it saucier?** Double the glaze, drizzle over rice or noodles when serving.
- **Dinner for one?** Halve everything, use one fillet, use a small tray. Same temps, same times.

## Pairs with

- Steamed jasmine or basmati rice (soaks up the glaze and the salmon fat drippings)
- Or rice noodles, or just crusty bread to mop the tray
- A cold lager, or a chilled dry white if it's the weekend
- The leftover glaze drizzled over tomorrow's lunch rice bowl
