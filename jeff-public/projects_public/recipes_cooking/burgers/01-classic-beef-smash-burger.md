# Classic Beef Smash Burger with American-Style Sauce

**Category:** burgers
**Source:** Jeff's, 2026-07-22
**Created:** 2026-07-22
**Last made:** —
**Rating:** —
**Servings:** 4
**Prep:** 10 min **Cook:** 10 min **Total:** 20 min

## Quick description

The proper American-style smash burger. Thin beef patties, smashed hard onto a screaming-hot griddle or pan so they get a proper crust. American cheese (or any melty cheese), pickles, onions, and a quick burger sauce. **The smash is the trick — high heat, hard press, no movement for 90 seconds. That's how you get the lacy, crispy edges.**

## Ingredients

### For the patties

- 500 g beef mince (20% fat is ideal — too lean = dry, too fatty = shrinking patty)
- Salt and black pepper
- 1 tbsp vegetable oil (for the griddle)

### For the American-style sauce

- 4 tbsp mayonnaise
- 2 tbsp tomato ketchup
- 1 tbsp American yellow mustard (or Dijon)
- 1 tbsp finely chopped pickled gherkins (or 1 tsp pickle brine)
- ½ tsp smoked paprika
- ½ tsp garlic powder

### To assemble

- 4 brioche buns (or sesame buns)
- 4 slices of American cheese (or cheddar, or any melty cheese)
- 1 small onion, finely sliced into rings
- Lettuce leaves
- Tomato slices
- Pickles (gherkins, sliced)
- Butter, for the buns

## Method

1. **Make the sauce.** Whisk all the sauce ingredients together in a small bowl. Set aside.
2. **Form the patties.** Divide the mince into 4 equal balls (about 125 g each). **Don't season yet** — salt draws out moisture and makes the smashed patty steam instead of sear. Don't form into patties either — you want balls so you can smash them.
3. **Heat the griddle or pan.** Get your griddle (or a heavy-based frying pan) **smoking hot**. Brush with vegetable oil.
4. **Smash.** Place a ball of mince on the griddle. Using a stiff metal spatula (or a second spatula pressed down on top), press down **really hard** to flatten the patty as thin as you can get it. **Hard, fast, 10 seconds.** Repeat with the other balls, working in batches. (You should get 2-3 patties per batch in a large griddle.)
5. **Season and sear.** Season the tops generously with salt and pepper. **Don't move the patties.** Let them cook for **90 seconds to 2 minutes** until the edges are lacy, brown, and crispy.
6. **Flip and cheese.** Scrape under each patty (the crispy bottom should release cleanly) and flip. Immediately place a slice of cheese on top. Cook for another 30-60 seconds until the cheese melts and the patty is just cooked through.
7. **Toast the buns.** Butter the cut sides of the buns. Toast them cut-side down on the griddle (or in a dry pan) for 30-60 seconds until golden.
8. **Assemble.** Sauce the bottom bun. Layer lettuce, tomato, the patty (with melted cheese), pickle slices, onion rings, more sauce, top bun. Eat immediately.

## Notes / variations

- **The smash is the entire game.** High heat + hard press + thin patty = the lacy, crispy edges that make this "lush." A thick patty on medium heat is just a sad hamburger, not a smash burger.
- **American cheese vs cheddar:** American cheese melts better (it has emulsifiers that stop it going grainy). Cheddar works but won't be as silky. A mix is fine.
- **Pickles are not optional.** They cut through the richness. Use gherkins if you can't get proper burger pickles.
- **Want it double?** Two patties per burger. Use smaller balls (60-70 g each). Smash them thinner. Stack with extra cheese.
- **No griddle?** A heavy-based frying pan on the highest heat works. The crust won't be quite as lacy but still good.
- **Form the balls ahead of time:** Up to 24 hours in the fridge, covered. Makes the smash step faster.

## Pairs with

- Oven chips (frozen, done properly at 220°C for 25 min)
- Coleslaw (cabbage + mayo + vinegar + sugar + salt — quick version)
- A cold beer or a milkshake if you're being properly American
- Pickles on the side, extra napkins
