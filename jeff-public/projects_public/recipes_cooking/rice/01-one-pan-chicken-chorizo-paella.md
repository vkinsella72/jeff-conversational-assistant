# One-Pan Chicken & Chorizo Paella

**Category:** rice
**Source:** Jeff's, 2026-07-22
**Created:** 2026-07-22
**Last made:** —
**Rating:** —
**Servings:** 4
**Prep:** 10 min **Cook:** 35 min **Total:** 45 min

## Quick description

The "fake-a-Spanish-holiday-in-Liverpool" rice. Chicken thighs, chorizo, and rice cooked in one wide pan with smoked paprika, saffron (or turmeric), stock, and frozen peas. **The trick is NOT stirring the rice once the stock goes in — that's how you get the crispy bottom layer (socarrat) that makes paella paella.**

## Ingredients

- 4 chicken thighs, bone-in, skin-on (or boneless if you prefer)
- 200 g cooking chorizo, sliced into 1 cm coins
- 300 g paella rice (or risotto rice, or even basmati in a pinch)
- 1 onion, finely chopped
- 1 red pepper, sliced
- 4 garlic cloves, crushed
- 1 x 400 g tin chopped tomatoes
- 750 ml hot chicken stock
- 1 tsp smoked paprika (hot or sweet)
- A pinch of saffron threads (or ½ tsp turmeric for the colour)
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- 200 g frozen peas
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- Salt and black pepper
- Lemon wedges, to serve
- Fresh parsley or dried parsley, to finish

## Method

1. **Brown the chicken and chorizo.** Heat the olive oil in a large wide frying pan (or actual paella pan if you've got one) over medium-high heat. Brown the chicken thighs skin-side down for 4-5 minutes until golden. Flip and brown the other side for 2 minutes. Transfer to a plate. Add the chorizo to the pan and cook for 2-3 minutes until it's released its paprika-red oil.
2. **Sauté the veg.** Add the onion and red pepper to the pan. Cook for 5-6 minutes until softening. Add the garlic, smoked paprika, saffron (or turmeric), and oregano. Cook for 1 minute.
3. **Add the tomatoes and rice.** Stir in the chopped tomatoes and the rice. Cook for 2 minutes, stirring, until the rice is coated and starting to turn translucent at the edges.
4. **Add the stock.** Pour in the hot stock. Return the chicken (and any juices) to the pan, nestling them into the rice. Season with salt and pepper.
5. **CRUCIAL: Do not stir from here on.** The rice needs to form a crust on the bottom of the pan. Stirring = mushy rice without socarrat. Let it bubble gently over medium-low heat for **20-25 minutes**, until the rice is cooked and the liquid is mostly absorbed.
6. **Add the peas** in the last 5 minutes of cooking, scattering them over the top (don't stir in).
7. **Form the socarrat.** When the rice is almost done and the liquid is mostly gone, turn the heat up to medium-high for the last 2-3 minutes. You'll hear a gentle crackling sound as the bottom layer crisps. **Smell it** — when it smells toasty (not burnt), it's done.
8. **Rest.** Remove from the heat, cover with a clean tea towel (not the lid — you want the steam to escape), and rest for 5 minutes.
9. **Serve** straight from the pan, with lemon wedges and a scatter of parsley.

## Notes / variations

- **The crispy bottom (socarrat) is the whole point of paella.** If you stir the rice after adding the stock, you'll never get it. Trust the no-stir method.
- **No paella rice?** Risotto rice (arborio) works — the texture will be slightly different (creamier) but still good. Basmati is the last-resort option.
- **No saffron?** Turmeric gives the colour but not the flavour. A pinch of smoked paprika adds back some depth. (Or just skip — it's still lush without it.)
- **Wider pan > deeper pan.** Paella wants to be a thin layer of rice in a wide pan so the liquid evaporates and the bottom crisps. A deep saucepan won't give you socarrat.
- **No chorizo?** 200 g diced pancetta or smoked bacon works. You lose the paprika-red oil though — add an extra teaspoon of smoked paprika.
- **Add seafood:** Mussels, prawns, and squid are classic. Add the prawns 5 min before the end, mussels with the peas (discard any that don't open).

## Pairs with

- A crisp green salad with a sharp dressing
- Crusty bread for mopping
- A cold Spanish lager or a glass of dry sherry
- Aioli (garlic mayo) on the side if you want to be properly Spanish
