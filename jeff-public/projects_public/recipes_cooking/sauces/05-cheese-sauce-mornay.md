# Cheese Sauce (Mornay, the Easy No-Lump Method)

**Category:** sauces
**Source:** Jeff's, 2026-07-22
**Created:** 2026-07-22
**Last made:** —
**Rating:** —
**Servings:** 4 (about 500 ml)
**Prep:** 5 min **Cook:** 10 min **Total:** 15 min

## Quick description

The 15-minute cheese sauce that doesn't go lumpy. Butter + flour + milk + cheese, made the right way. **The trick is warm milk (not cold) added gradually while whisking, and taking the pan off the heat before adding the cheese.**

## Ingredients

- 50 g butter
- 50 g plain flour
- 500 ml milk (whole is best, but any works)
- 150 g grated cheese (cheddar, gruyère, or a mix)
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tsp Worcestershire sauce (optional)
- Pinch of grated nutmeg (optional)
- Salt and black pepper

## Method

1. **Warm the milk.** Pour the milk into a saucepan and warm it over low heat until it's just steaming (not boiling). This is the trick — warm milk incorporates into the roux without going lumpy.
2. **Make the roux.** In a separate saucepan, melt the butter over medium heat. Add the flour and whisk constantly for 1-2 minutes — it should bubble and turn a pale gold. This cooks out the raw flour taste.
3. **Add the milk.** Pour in the warm milk a little at a time, whisking constantly with a balloon whisk (or a regular whisk — but balloon is best). Start with a splash, whisk until smooth, then add more. Once you've added about a third, you can add the rest more confidently.
4. **Simmer.** Bring to a gentle simmer, whisking, for 2-3 minutes until the sauce thickens to coat the back of a spoon.
5. **Add the cheese.** Take the pan off the heat. Add the grated cheese, Dijon, Worcestershire, nutmeg (if using), salt, and pepper. Whisk until the cheese is fully melted and the sauce is smooth and glossy.
6. **Taste and adjust.** The cheese is salty, so you may not need much salt. Add more cheese for a thicker sauce, more milk for thinner.

## Notes / variations

- **The warm-milk trick is the no-lump insurance.** Cold milk + hot roux = lumpy mess. Warm milk + hot roux = silky sauce.
- **The cheese goes in off the heat.** Adding cheese to a boiling sauce can make it go stringy or split. Off the heat = smooth, glossy melt.
- **Use any melty cheese.** Cheddar is classic. Gruyère is more sophisticated. A mix is the best of both. Parmesan stirred in at the end adds a salty bite.
- **Want it spicier?** Add a pinch of cayenne or a teaspoon of mustard powder with the flour.
- **Use as:** Mac and cheese sauce (pour over cooked macaroni, top with more cheese, bake), lasagne white sauce, cauliflower cheese sauce, the base for any pasta bake.
- **Storage:** Keeps in the fridge for 3 days. Reheat gently with a splash of milk to loosen.

## Pairs with

- Mac and cheese (the obvious one)
- Cauliflower cheese
- Lasagne
- Fish pie
- Pouring over steamed vegetables
