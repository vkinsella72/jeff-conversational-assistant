# One-Pan Sausage & Tomato Penne

**Category:** pasta
**Source:** Jeff's, 2026-07-22
**Created:** 2026-07-22
**Last made:** —
**Rating:** —
**Servings:** 4
**Prep:** 5 min **Cook:** 25 min **Total:** 30 min

## Quick description

The weeknight pasta that everyone should have in their back pocket. Italian sausages browned and broken up, tinned tomatoes, dried oregano, garlic, and penne — all cooked in one pan. **No draining, no separate sauce pan, the pasta cooks in the tomato sauce and absorbs all the flavour.**

## Ingredients

- 6 good pork sausages (or 4 if they're the big ones)
- 1 onion, finely chopped
- 4 garlic cloves, crushed
- 1 x 400 g tin chopped tomatoes
- 1 tbsp tomato purée
- 500 ml chicken or vegetable stock
- 300 g penne pasta
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- 1 tsp dried chilli flakes (optional)
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- Salt and black pepper
- Parmesan, to finish
- Fresh basil or dried oregano, to finish

## Method

1. **Brown the sausages.** Heat the olive oil in a large deep frying pan or saucepan over medium-high heat. Squeeze the sausage meat out of the casings (or use them whole and break up later). Cook for 5-6 minutes, breaking it up with a wooden spoon, until well-browned.
2. **Soften the aromatics.** Add the onion to the pan and cook for 4-5 minutes until softening. Add the garlic, oregano, and chilli flakes, cook for 1 minute.
3. **Add tomatoes and stock.** Stir in the tinned tomatoes, tomato purée, and stock. Season with salt and pepper. Bring to a vigorous simmer.
4. **Add the pasta.** Tip in the penne, stir to make sure it's mostly submerged. Reduce the heat to medium so it simmers gently.
5. **Cook uncovered for 12-15 minutes**, stirring every couple of minutes to stop the pasta sticking to the bottom. The pasta should be al dente and most of the liquid should be absorbed, leaving a glossy sauce clinging to the pasta. If it looks dry before the pasta is done, add a splash more stock.
6. **Finish.** Taste and adjust seasoning. Scatter with grated parmesan and more dried oregano.

## Notes / variations

- **The pasta cooks in the sauce — that's the trick.** No draining, no second pan. The starch from the pasta thickens the sauce; the sauce flavours the pasta. They make each other better.
- **Spicier?** Use hot Italian sausages, or add a diced fresh chilli with the garlic.
- **Creamy version:** Stir 100 ml double cream in at the end. Different vibe, also lush.
- **Add greens:** Toss in 200 g frozen spinach in the last 5 minutes, or a handful of frozen peas.
- **No sausages?** Use 400 g chicken thigh pieces, diced and browned, or 500 g prawns (added in the last 5 min).

## Pairs with

- Crusty bread (if you've got any, though you said bread doesn't last — feel free to skip)
- A green salad with a sharp vinaigrette
- A glass of red wine
- The next day's leftovers are better — the sauce has had time to soak in
